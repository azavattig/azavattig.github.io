DealNexa

The problem

Buy-side deal teams spend weeks reading a seller's data room: financial statements, contracts, rosters, leases, legal files. The risks that matter are often spread across several documents that contradict each other, and they surface late, after the price is set.

What I built

DealNexa is a deal-management platform for private equity and venture capital teams. I co-founded it and built its due-diligence engine: it parses the data room, runs Claude over the documents in batches to extract and rank risks, writes the diligence report, and feeds deterministic QoE, risk-scoring and LBO models. I ran product and operations, including the CRM and ERP workflows.

About the demo. This is DealNexa's own React frontend, built from the product's code and running in your browser in demo mode. Sign-in is skipped and a small fake backend answers the app's requests from saved data, so nothing reaches a database or an AI model. The company is fictional: Ridgeline Roofing, from DealNexa's sample data room of 17 documents. The diligence report was produced by running DealNexa's prompts and report format over those documents with Claude, then saved. The LBO runs live through a port of DealNexa's engine. Real organizations and people named in the sample documents are replaced with generic labels. Changes you make reset when the page reloads.

Use the app

You're signed in as a demo analyst at a buyout firm reviewing Ridgeline Roofing. Start on the deal dashboard, with its live risk score and LBO, then jump to another screen or click around the app freely.
