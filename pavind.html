Route optimization · Pavind S.r.l. · Sulmona, Italy · 2025–2026

Pavind

The problem

A municipal road-cleaning fleet in Sulmona works day and night shifts. Every job has a time window, every truck has a capacity, and each job needs a specific vehicle type. Each day someone has to decide which truck does which jobs, in what order, without missing a window or pushing a crew into overtime.

What I built

I modeled the day as a capacitated vehicle routing problem with time windows (CVRPTW) and solved it with Google OR-Tools, one model per shift. A forecasting layer (Prophet and LightGBM) estimated upcoming demand, and real day/night shift rules shaped the schedule.

Result in live operations

15% less route distance

12% fewer idle fleet hours

About the demo. The original system and its data are confidential. What the model assumes
  • Time on site comes from published ranges, not Pavind's records: skip pickup 15–30 min, suction 45–150 min (septic and sewer pumping runs 30 min to 2 h, plus jetting), sludge loading 20–50 min, and motorway sweeping by stretch length at 8–12 km/h, one carriageway per job.
  • Breaks are random for every driver and day, the way crews really take them: 40% have coffee before the shift (10–30 min), 60% stop for coffee mid-morning (10–45 min), everyone takes lunch (30–120 min, starting 12:00–14:30), and night crews take one 20–60 min break. A 45-minute lunch also covers the EU rule of a 45-minute break after 4.5 h of driving.
  • Unloading: a truck can drive back to the yard to tip its load (20–25 min) and keep working. The solver decides when the detour is worth it.
  • Overtime of up to 2 hours is allowed, at a high cost, only when it is the only way to serve a job. Late finishes are allowed the same way.
  • Roads: real road distances and routes from OpenStreetMap (via OSRM); trucks drive 25% slower than cars.
